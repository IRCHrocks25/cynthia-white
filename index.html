The Great Reframe — Grief Is Energy, Not Pathology

Grief Is Not a Stage to Complete. It Is Energy to Move.

Were you trained on the five stages of grief? Denial, anger, bargaining, depression, acceptance?

Cynthia studied directly under the woman who wrote them, Elisabeth Kübler-Ross. Even she never meant those stages to guide grief work with children. They were built to describe the process of dying, not the process of grieving.

An illness is diagnosed and managed. Energy is met, exactly as it shows up.

7.4%

In GriefCynergy's own practitioner survey, only 7.4% of respondents said they had a clear, professional framework for supporting a grieving or traumatized child. Most are relying on instinct, not training.

Grief does not move in steps. It moves like energy, looking for somewhere to go.

Big energy.

Defiance. Movement. A raised voice.

The energy you were trained to correct.

Small energy.

Silence. Stillness. A child who will not meet your eyes.

The energy you were trained to worry about.

Both are the same thing, asking for the same response: an adult who knows how to meet it, not manage it.

Add trauma, and the energy becomes chaotic. How you regulate your own energy matters.

“We do not seek to change people. We create the conditions where remembering their authentic selves becomes possible.” Cynthia L. White

The Skill of Joining — Five Steps to Attunement

Something You Can Actually Do

Presence is not abstract. It is the Skill of Joining: five specific, teachable actions, in order, that any trusted adult can practice under pressure. This is how you regulate the flow of energy.

01

Say What You See.

Ground the moment in plain, judgment-free observation. "You are jumping. You are sitting quietly. You made a big circle with a yellow crayon."

02

Say What You Hear.

Reflect the child's own words back to them, with the same tone, so presence becomes something they can feel, not just something you feel.

03

Match the Energy.

Meet big energy or small energy on its own terms, instead of steering it toward whatever is easier for you.

04

Play Along with the Child.

Follow their lead into their world of play, instead of redirecting them into yours.

05

Share Small Parts of Your Own Story.

Let them see a person, not a technique. This is what turns a trained adult into a trusted one.

Two Childhood Wounds, One Proven Blueprint

Where the Torch Was Lit

At six years old, Cynthia stepped off a sidewalk, and for a moment, the world went still. The sound gone. The birds silent. A presence stopped her in place and left her with a message that would shape the rest of her life: everything that happens is right.

Soon after, her father drove her mother away and left his children behind. Cynthia moved through a series of foster homes. Three of them were abusive.

It was the fourth home, with a family named Riggs, where she felt unconditional love for the first time. No lectures, no techniques, just a family that showed up, fully, for her life. That love was steady enough to pull her back into herself, and she has spent 45 years teaching adults how to give a child what the Riggs family gave her.

Hiro's story runs a parallel path. He lost his mother before he turned three. A Catholic monastery took him in first, and for a while, he knew that same kind of love. Then he was moved to a second orphanage in Japan, where the abuse he survived was severe enough that parts of his own childhood are simply gone.

He carried that loss quietly into adulthood, until he trained under Cynthia and recognized, in his own body, what he had been silently suffering all along.

Neither of them started with a theory. Both started with a wound, and with someone who showed up. That is the origin of everything GriefCynergy now teaches.

Dismantling the Caregiver's Silent Objections

Are You the Trusted Adult We Mean?

“Healing only happens in relationships of equality.” Terry Real

This training is not for you if you want a clinical shortcut, a script that removes all uncertainty, or a certificate that does not change how you actually show up for a child.

It is for you if you are ready to trade the freeze for something you can practice, and get better at, one child at a time.

Is this backed by real science, or is it just a feeling?

Both. The five components of Joining are grounded in attachment theory, post-traumatic growth theory, and polyvagal theory. A child's nervous system registers whether an adult is regulated before a single word is spoken. And growth after trauma is not the same as recovery or positive thinking. It does not erase what happened. It grows alongside it, and only when a trusted adult stays close enough for that to happen.

What does this cost?

The introductory webinar is free. Organizational training is shaped to the size of your team and the setting you work in, whether that is a school, a hospital, or a disaster response unit. Book a conversation, and we will build a plan that fits.

Do I need a clinical degree to learn this?

No. Joining was built for trusted adults first: teachers, foster parents, volunteers, and clergy, not only therapists and social workers. If you are the adult a child turns to, this training is built for you.
